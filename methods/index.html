01 — Foundation

Analytical scope

The portal organises 427 included publications on artificial intelligence in ophthalmology. Its public data layer contains bibliographic metadata, publication-level classifications, dataset links, evidence-map assignments, and aggregate visualisations.

The review has an imaging-dependent scope centred on OCT, OCTA, and related ophthalmic imaging. Categories describe the clinical, technical, translational, ethical, and governance themes represented across the included literature.

Three reviewers (T.S., M.F., and R.V.) applied the eligibility criteria, with at least two authors involved in each decision and disagreements or borderline cases resolved by consensus. Searches covered PubMed, ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, and Scopus through 30 April 2026, without a lower publication-date limit.

Assignments required explicit, unambiguous evidence linking AI to both the dimension and the assigned framing. Absent or ambiguous evidence remained unassigned.

02 — Operational language

Coding definitions

!
Interpretation boundary

Evidence-map categories describe publication-level framing. They are not pooled effect estimates, risk-of-bias judgements, or certainty ratings, and multiple assignments may apply to one publication.

Operational meanings used by the general and ethics evidence maps
MapCategoryOperational meaning
GeneralPositiveThe publication frames AI as enabling or improving the named dimension.
GeneralNegativeThe publication frames AI as creating or exposing a barrier, risk, failure, or constraint.
GeneralBalancedThe publication provides explicit qualifying evidence in both directions within the same dimension.
EthicsCausedAI is described as introducing or amplifying the issue.
EthicsResolvedA reported AI-related mechanism mitigates the issue.
EthicsBothThe publication provides explicit evidence that AI both creates and mitigates the issue.

Multiple assignments per publication are permitted. Assignment totals therefore describe the distribution of analytical categories and can exceed the number of included publications.

03 — Reading the evidence

Interpretation

General map

Direction of framing

Positive, negative, and balanced categories describe whether a dimension is presented as enabling, limiting, or explicitly qualified in both directions.

Ethics map

Role attributed to AI

Caused, resolved, and both categories distinguish whether AI is framed as introducing an issue, mitigating it through an AI-related mechanism, or doing both.

These assignments support structured comparison across the literature. They do not represent effect estimates, study-quality ratings, or certainty assessments.

04 — Boundaries

Limitations

The evidence base is heterogeneous in publication type, clinical indication, AI task, imaging modality, dataset, validation strategy, and outcome reporting. The dashboard supports descriptive exploration rather than meta-analysis.

The imaging-dependent and English-language scope limits generalisation beyond the represented literature. Publication-level assignments remain interpretive even when operational definitions are explicit.

The review did not assess study-level risk of bias. The coding audit strengthens traceability, but the counts do not establish efficacy. Limited reporting of demographic and socioeconomic characteristics constrained assessment of population coverage and equity.
